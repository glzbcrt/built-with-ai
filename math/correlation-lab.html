THE 30-SECOND GUIDE

Meet the correlation coefficient.

Pearson’s r measures the direction and strength of a linear relationship. It ranges from −1 to +1.

−1 Move in opposite directions0 No linear pattern+1 Move together

Strength is about distance from zero. A negative correlation can be just as strong as a positive one.

A pattern is a starting point. Correlation alone never proves cause and effect. Confounding variables, shared trends, chance, and outliers can all create misleading patterns.

In this lab: |r| ≥ 0.70 is “strong”; |r| ≤ 0.30 is “weak”; values between are “moderate.” These are teaching conventions, not universal rules. A weak Pearson correlation can hide a strong curved relationship.
