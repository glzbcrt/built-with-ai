A FIELD GUIDE TO THE SHAPES

Look beyond the average.

The same average can hide very different stories. Watch the shape, center, and spread together.

01 / NORMAL

The familiar bell

Values cluster near the center, with fewer toward either end. A normal distribution is symmetric; in an ideal normal model, about 68% of values fall within one standard deviation of the mean.

02 / UNIFORM

An even playing field

Equal-width intervals are equally likely in a uniform model. A finite random sample may look uneven. Our sample is deliberately balanced to make the flat shape easy to see.

03 / SKEWED

A tail tells a story

A right-skewed distribution has a longer tail toward larger values. High values pull the mean upward; the median is usually less affected. Income and waiting times often show this pattern.

04 / BIMODAL

Two peaks, two groups?

Two clusters can create two peaks. That may suggest a mixture of groups or processes, but the shape alone does not prove why. Bin choices can reveal or obscure those peaks.

Try this: Load the skewed sample and add 200. Compare the mean and median. Then change the bin count—your data stays the same, but its visual story changes.
